## About

I started in electrical engineering — circuits, microcontrollers, signal processing — and found that the
problems I liked most were the ones about *moving and making sense of data*. That took me into Python,
SQL, and Azure, where I now spend my time building pipelines and analytics systems.

The hardware background still shows up in how I work: I like understanding a system down to the layer
below the one I'm working in, and I'd rather measure something than guess at it.

**What I bring to a team**

- 🔧 **End-to-end ownership** — from ingestion and transformation through to the queries and dashboards people actually use
- ☁️ **Cloud-native by default** — Azure primarily, with AWS exposure; containerized, reproducible, version-controlled
- 🧭 **Cross-domain fluency** — comfortable talking to hardware engineers and data consumers in the same meeting
- 🤖 **AI-native workflow** — see below; this is where most of my learning energy goes right now

## What I'm Focused On

I'm working on moving from *reactive* AI pair-programming to *orchestrated* AI engineering.

The day-to-day version of that is a chat-and-iterate loop across **Codex**, **Claude**, and **GitHub Copilot**
inside VS Code Insiders. The direction I'm pushing it is:

- **Automated workflows** — agents that carry a task from spec to PR rather than one suggestion at a time
- **Event-driven triggers** — work that kicks off from a commit, an alert, or a schedule instead of from me
- **Scheduled tasks** — recurring analysis, checks, and reports that run without a human in the loop

I treat my side projects as the proving ground for this: the goal isn't the app, it's the workflow that ships it.

Most of that proving ground lives in private repos — **Flare**, a SwiftUI meetup app on Firebase with a
TypeScript Cloud Functions backend; **Durak**, an offline card game in Expo with its own rules engine and
bots; **Restore Old Photos**, a Next.js platform running Gemini on Vertex AI behind Stripe credits and a
Cloud Run worker; a resident-only portal for a condo building; **Keasy**, a real-estate marketing monorepo;
and **Command Center**, a local-first dashboard that pulls Strava, Garmin, Gmail and Calendar into one view
and runs a rules engine over the result. The public ones are smaller and more fun:
[Walkout Music](https://github.com/SavaStosic3/Walkout-Music) plays a guest's entrance song off an NFC tap,
[SEO Search Tool](https://github.com/SavaStosic3/SEO-Search-Tool) bulk-audits Core Web Vitals from a CSV of
URLs, and [Mini Utilities](https://github.com/SavaStosic3/Mini-Utilities) is exactly what it sounds like.
